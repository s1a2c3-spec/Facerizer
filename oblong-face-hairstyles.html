Hairstyles, Glasses and Beard Styles for an Oblong Face

Updated 2026-10-21

An oblong (or rectangle) face is noticeably longer than it is wide. The forehead, cheeks and jaw are similar in width, and the cheek lines are fairly straight.

The goal with an oblong face is to add width at the sides and reduce the look of length. Use the ideas below as a starting point, and remember that every face shape looks good with the right cut. Your hair type and your own taste matter more than any rule.

How to tell if you have an oblong face

  • Your face is clearly longer than it is wide, by more than 1.5 times.
  • Your forehead, cheeks and jaw are close in width.
  • Your cheek lines are long and fairly straight.

Look straight into a mirror with your hair pulled back, or use a clear front-facing photo. Compare the width of your forehead, cheekbones and jaw, and the length from hairline to chin. See how to measure your face shape, or let Facerizer measure it from your photo.

Hairstyles for an oblong face

Worth styling with care: Very tall quiffs, long straight hair parted in the middle and very short sides with lots of height can add to the length. They can still be great if that is the look you want.

Glasses for an oblong face

  • Often work well: Oversized, round and square frames with deeper lenses.
  • Also worth trying: Frames with detail on the sides or the top bar add width.
  • Keep in mind: Very small, narrow frames can make the face look longer.

Beard and facial hair for an oblong face

  • A fuller beard on the sides adds width.
  • Keep the chin area shorter. A long, pointed goatee adds length.

Facial hair is optional. A clean-shaven look suits every face shape, and you should wear whatever you are comfortable with.

Quick tips

  • Think width: fringe, waves and side volume.
  • Keep height on top moderate.
  • Deeper lenses help balance a long face.

Common questions about oblong faces

What hairstyle suits a long or oblong face?

Styles that add width and a shorter look, such as curtains, a jaw-length bob, waves with side volume, or a short fringe, suit a long face well.

Should I avoid long hair with an oblong face?

Not necessarily. Layers that add width at the sides work well. Very long, flat and straight hair can add to the length.

What glasses suit an oblong face?

Oversized, round or square frames with deeper lenses add width and balance the length.
