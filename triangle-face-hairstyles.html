Hairstyles, Glasses and Beard Styles for a Triangle Face

Updated 2026-10-21

A triangle (or pear) face has a jaw that is wider than the forehead. The top of the face is narrower and the lower face is fuller.

The goal with a triangle face is to add volume at the top and soften the jaw area. Use the ideas below as a starting point, and remember that every face shape looks good with the right cut. Your hair type and your own taste matter more than any rule.

How to tell if you have a triangle face

  • Your jaw is the widest part of your face.
  • Your forehead is narrower than your jaw.
  • Your cheekbones are less prominent than your jaw.

Look straight into a mirror with your hair pulled back, or use a clear front-facing photo. Compare the width of your forehead, cheekbones and jaw, and the length from hairline to chin. See how to measure your face shape, or let Facerizer measure it from your photo.

Hairstyles for a triangle face

Worth styling with care: Heavy volume at jaw level and blunt chin-length cuts that stop at the widest point add to the lower width. They can still look good if you like them.

Glasses for a triangle face

  • Often work well: Browline, cat-eye and frames with detail at the top.
  • Also worth trying: Light colours on the lower frame look balanced.
  • Keep in mind: Heavy, wide bottom frames add to the width of the jaw.

Beard and facial hair for a triangle face

  • Keep a light stubble or a short, neat beard.
  • A very full beard along the jaw adds to the width there.

Facial hair is optional. A clean-shaven look suits every face shape, and you should wear whatever you are comfortable with.

Quick tips

  • Think volume on top and lighter sides.
  • A fringe or side sweep adds width at the forehead.
  • Frames that draw attention to the eyes help balance.

Common questions about triangle faces

What hairstyle suits a triangle face?

Styles with volume on top, such as a quiff, a textured crown, a side-swept fringe or a top knot, balance a wider jaw.

What glasses suit a triangle face?

Browline and cat-eye frames, and frames with detail at the top, add width to the upper face and balance the jaw.

Should I avoid a beard with a wide jaw?

Not at all. Keep it short and neat so it does not add extra width along the jaw.
